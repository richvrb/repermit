# UAE Fire and Life Safety Code of Practice: official availability and use as a RePermit RAG source

Status as of 1 November 2026 (all URL tests were run that day). Evidence labels: **[PRIMARY-READ]** = government document whose text was actually read. **[PRIMARY-SNIPPET]** = government page seen only as a search-engine summary, not opened. **[LEAD]** = secondary source, not evidence. **[NOT VERIFIED]** = could not be confirmed.

**Most important limitation: the text of the code itself was not obtained or read.** Everything below about its content is taken from other government documents that cite it.

## What is the latest edition, and are there amendments, circulars or a newer edition up to November 2026?

### Takeaway
The September 2018 edition is the latest edition that can be evidenced; no primary or secondary source shows a newer full edition up to November 2026. This is an absence of evidence, not proof, because the DCD site could not be read. Dubai authorities keep referring to the "latest version" without naming a year, so RePermit should record "2018, to be confirmed with DCD".

- The "2026 edition" claims look like marketing or confusion with the Abu Dhabi amendments.
- The Abu Dhabi amendments should not be ingested as applicable to Dubai.
- DBC 2021 states that where an element of the FLSC does not apply in Dubai this is indicated in the DBC text ([DBC 2021](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2021/12/Dubai%20Building%20Code_English_2021%20Edition_compressed.pdf), A.6), so Dubai deviations may exist beyond the code text.

## Which chapters cover existing buildings, change of use, renovation, alterations, fire separations and egress, and what does the code say about modifications?

### Takeaway
Chapters 1, 2 and 3 are verified indirectly through DBC 2021 citations (sections and tables in those chapters), with Chapter 3 clearly egress. **No chapter dedicated to existing buildings, change of use or alterations could be verified.** The best available primary rule for modifications is DBC Table A.1, which applies the DBC and FLSC by type of change.

### Cited Findings
- [PRIMARY-READ] DBC A.4.3: "The DBC and UAE Fire and Life Safety Code of Practice (UAE FLSC) together form the main regulations for building design in Dubai" — [DBC 2021](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2021/12/Dubai%20Building%20Code_English_2021%20Edition_compressed.pdf)
- [PRIMARY-READ] DBC A.5.1 and Table A.1 (same source; right column partly truncated in the extraction, so check against the PDF before quoting):

| Change | DBC description | Where DBC + UAE FLSC apply |
|---|---|---|
| Renovation | Linings/finishes or strengthening of structure, no reconfiguration of internal spaces | Interior finish or envelope changes |
| Modification | Fit-out of a space/unit, or change to a service system or building element | The fit-out or change |
| Reconstruction | Modification that is not stand-alone; affects adjacent egress routes or systems outside the area | The reconstruction and impacted areas |
| Change of use | Change of use or occupancy of part or all of the building | The change and impacted areas, e.g. existing exit stairs and routes, vertical transport, sanitary provisions |
| Addition | Increase in gross/built-up area, height or depth | The addition; if it relies on the existing building for escape, vertical transport etc., the existing building shall also be assessed |

- [PRIMARY-READ] A.5.1 allows an exception where an alternative solution is permitted (A.8) — [DBC 2021](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2021/12/Dubai%20Building%20Code_English_2021%20Edition_compressed.pdf)
- [PRIMARY-READ] Chapter references found in DBC 2021 (edition 2018), same source:
  - Ch. 3: exits and travel. Section 3.10 (points of exit discharge context), Section 4 (exit stairs), Section 5 (minimum corridor widths), Table 3.4, Table 3.8 (exit passageway widths), Table 3.13 (occupant load factors).
  - Ch. 1: Table 1.9 (fire pump rooms, and a fire separation conforming to Table 1.9 for parking), Section 7.1.40, and "treated as a mall in accordance with Ch. 1".
  - Ch. 2: Section 2 and Section 2.8 (fire service vehicle access context).
  - The DBC also cites Ch. 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13 and 14 without titles being established here.
- [PRIMARY-READ] Fire safety during modification: "Building owner, Consultant and Contractor are responsible for implementation of fire safety during construction, modification, alteration and demolition activities"; fire risk assessments, hot-work permits, and safe access and egress are required — [DDA Circular 333](https://dda.gov.ae/-/media/638CE9B231D844848FFA84D11CCF495D.pdf); repeated with enforcement language in [DDA Circular 667](https://dda.gov.ae/-/media/4B489270690D4C8293B5642765469D2B.pdf)
- [LEAD] One vendor blog says existing buildings need not retrofit unless they undergo fit-out, change of use or add new fire risks — [mmjss](https://mmjss.com/whats-changed-and-who-it-affects-uae-fire-life-safety-code-2026/). Not verified.
- [LEAD] Chapter structure conflicts in secondary sources: Chapter 1 as construction and compartmentation, 2 as access, 3 as egress, 4 as extinguishers, versus another summary with Chapter 2 as occupancy classification; counts of 18, 20 and 26 chapters are reported. These may reflect different editions.

### Inferences
- Chapter 1 likely holds construction and fire separation requirements and Chapter 2 fire service access. This is inferred from the DBC's citations and is compatible with the leads, but the chapter titles were not read.
- For RePermit, Table A.1 can already drive a classification step (renovation / modification / reconstruction / change of use / addition), and "reconstruction" and "change of use" are the triggers that extend scope beyond the works area. This is DBC text, not FLSC text.

## What are the terms of reuse or copyright, and may it be stored in a commercial knowledge base?

### Takeaway
No official reuse licence for the code was found, and nothing authorises storage in a commercial knowledge base. Treat it as **not cleared**: seek written permission from MOI/DCD and get UAE legal advice before ingesting the full text. Note that the DBC, another likely RAG source, has an explicit no-copying clause.

### Cited Findings
- [PRIMARY-READ] DBC A.10 (Copyright): "No content of the DBC, in part or whole, shall be copied, printed, sold or reproduced in any format." — [DBC 2021](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2021/12/Dubai%20Building%20Code_English_2021%20Edition_compressed.pdf). The DM page also states "Copyrights belong to the Government of Dubai – All Rights Reserved" — [DM DBC page](https://www.dm.gov.ae/municipality-business/planning-and-construction/dubai-building-code-2/)
- [PRIMARY-READ] MOI offers the code through a purchase service, which shows it is distributed commercially as well as having been posted on DCD — [MOI directory](https://portal.moi.gov.ae/eservices/default.aspx?Culture=en)
- [PRIMARY-READ] DM's website terms, as summarised on fetch, contain no specific licence for reproduction or commercial reuse of content — [DM terms](https://www.dm.gov.ae/website-policies/terms-and-conditions/)
- [PRIMARY-SNIPPET] Article 3 of Federal Decree-Law 38/2021 reportedly excludes official documents such as laws, regulations, resolutions and judgments from protection. This comes from a search summary only; the law text itself could not be opened — [uaelegislation](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1534/download); [MoET PDF](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/376326/copyright.pdf/1b4d5d16-8e3c-6012-afa8-56cd4eb008da?t=1645596129916)

### Inferences
- Whether Article 3 covers the code is a legal question. The code is a technical document and is sold by the ministry, and a search summary of press coverage says it is largely based on NFPA codes ([LEAD], article not identified, so third-party rights are an open question). These facts point against assuming it is free of copyright. This is not legal advice.
- Lower-risk interim design for the demo: store bibliographic metadata, section references and short self-written summaries, and let the user check the licensed text; hold back full-text ingestion until permission exists.
- The same clearance is needed for the DBC.

## How does Dubai Civil Defence issue NOCs for modifications?

### Takeaway
The legal basis is firm: owners must notify and get approval for modifications that may affect preventive safety. The operational details are not verified because DCD's service pages were unreachable. DDA requires a DCD NOC "if applicable" without defining the trigger. **"Al Dhaman" could not be confirmed as a DCD system in any source**, so RePermit should not name it.

### Cited Findings
- [PRIMARY-READ] Law 4/2025, Art. 17(4): owners must "notify the CDGC within the time frame it determines, and obtain its approval, regarding any modification to the Establishment, Facility, or Building that may affect the Preventive Safety Requirements" — [Law 4/2025](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2025/Law%20No.%20(4)%20of%202025%20Establishing%20the%20Dubai%20Civil%20Defence%20General%20Command.pdf)

- [PRIMARY-READ] Art. 31 (transitional paragraph; article number inferred from its position before Art. 32) keeps existing Civil Defence legislation in force to the extent it does not contradict the Law. ### Inferences
- The Art. 17(4) test ("may affect the Preventive Safety Requirements") is a usable legal trigger for RePermit, and "Preventive Safety Requirements" is broadly defined in the Law as requirements for protection against fire hazard, fire prevention, firefighting, and disaster response. The Law does not name the UAE FLSC; the link between them is inferred from the government documents above.
- Terms differ by jurisdiction: DM areas and DDA zones have separate permit services, so RePermit should record the competent authority per plot. DBC Table A.1 and Law 4/2025 apply across authorities, whereas the DDA documents apply only to DDA areas.
